# Reference claims — Evans_1973

Source: `Evans_1973_The Crystal Structures of Cavansite and Pentagonite.pdf`
("The Crystal Structures of Cavansite and Pentagonite", Howard T. Evans, Jr.,
American Mineralogist 58, 412–424, 1973).

Written from the source text only. **Caveat:** outputs had been seen before
drafting (same as the other claim files).

1. Cavansite and pentagonite are dimorphs of Ca(VO)(Si₄O₁₀)·4H₂O.
2. They were found in Malheur County, Oregon (a roadcut near Owyhee Dam, 1960).
3. Both are orthorhombic.
4. Both represent novel layer-silicate structure types.
5. Both contain zigzag, pyroxene-like (SiO₃)ₙ chains joined laterally into
   sheets, with tetrahedral apices pointing alternately plus and minus along b.
6. Cavansite's lateral linkage forms a network of 4-fold and 8-fold rings.
7. Pentagonite's network is entirely 6-fold rings.
8. The vanadyl (VO) groups and Ca²⁺ ions lie in mirror planes between the
   silicate layers.
9. Vanadium is in square-pyramidal coordination and Ca is 7-fold coordinated,
   in both structures.
10. H₂O molecules are poorly resolved, have high apparent thermal parameters,
    and are probably zeolitic.
11. The structures favour twinning in pentagonite (commonly fiveling groups) but
    prohibit twinning in cavansite (twinning not observed there).
12. Refinement R values are 0.109 (cavansite) and 0.081 (pentagonite); bond
    lengths carry estimated errors of ±0.02 Å.

Note: both models reported many details *beyond* these claims (Si–O averages
1.627/1.6004 Å, V valence sums 4.33/4.46, tetrahedral angles 106.8°/112.0°,
the rejected name "redstarite", "one-dimensional in pentagonite", "concentric
prismatic tubes") — all verified present in the source. The claim set is
abstract-level, so coverage under-counts a detailed-but-unfocused summary.
