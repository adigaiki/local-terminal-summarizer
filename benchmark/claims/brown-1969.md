# Reference claims — Brown_1969

Source: `Brown_1969_OXYGEN COORDINATION AND THE Si-O BOND.pdf`
("Oxygen coordination and the Si-O bond", G. E. Brown & G. V. Gibbs).

Written from the source text only. **Caveat:** the first outputs had already
been seen before these claims were drafted, so the cold-start condition was
broken for this document. A fresh reader should re-derive these before treating
the scores as definitive.

1. The grand mean Si–O distance increases with the mean coordination number
   (C.N.) of oxygen bonded to silicon: about 1.608 Å at C.N. 2 and 1.638 Å at
   C.N. 4.
2. The correlation coefficient for that relation is 0.78, i.e. about 60% of the
   variation is explained by a linear dependence.
3. The dataset is 46 three-dimensionally refined structures.
4. In refinements of low cordierite and beryl, Si–O bonds to two-coordinated
   oxygen (~1.595 Å) are shorter than to three-coordinated oxygen (~1.625 Å).
5. Feldspar and zeolite data were excluded because their average oxygen
   coordination number could not be determined unambiguously.
6. Na-silicate data (Na₂SiO₃, α-Na₂Si₂O₅, β-Na₂SiO₃) were excluded because
   strong d-p π-bonding overrides the coordination effect.
7. In four C2/m amphiboles, longer Si–O(nbr) bonds are associated with higher
   average electronegativity of the nontetrahedral cations bonded to O(nbr).
8. Glaucophane's Si–O(nbr) bonds are about 0.01 Å longer on average than
   corresponding bonds in tremolite.
9. The tremolite exception — Si–O(nbr) bonds to three- and four-coordinated
   oxygens can be shorter than Si–O(br) bonds — is explained by Cruickshank's
   (1961) d-p π-bonding theory.
10. The coordination-number trend is explained in part by isovalent
    hybridization: longer Si–O bonds reflect less s-character in the bond.
11. The work was supported in part by National Science Foundation Grant GA-1133.
12. Morimoto and Koto (1969) reported a decrease in mean Si–O distances with
    increasing Mg→Fe replacement in the orthoenstatite–orthoferrosilite series.
